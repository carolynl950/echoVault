# Normal distribution

Bell-shaped, symmetric, described completely by the mean μ and standard deviation σ.

## 68-95-99.7 rule

- About 68% of values are within 1σ of the mean
- About 95% are within 2σ
- About 99.7% are within 3σ

Example: SAT math with mean 520 and σ 115. About 95% of scores fall between 290 and 750.

## z-scores

z = (x − μ) / σ, which is how many standard deviations from the mean. It lets you compare things on different scales. I got 82 on the stats midterm (mean 74, σ 6) → z = 1.33. I got 88 on the bio midterm (mean 85, σ 9) → z = 0.33. So the stats score was relatively better even though the number is lower.

## Central Limit Theorem

Take lots of samples and compute each sample's mean. The distribution of those means is roughly normal if n is big enough (around 30 or more), even if the population itself isn't normal. Its spread is σ/√n, so bigger samples give more precise means. This is the reason so many tests assume normality.
