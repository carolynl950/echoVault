# Sampling and bias

Population = everyone you care about. Sample = the people you actually measure. A statistic describes the sample, a parameter describes the population.

## Sampling methods

- Simple random sample: everyone has an equal chance. The gold standard.
- Stratified: split into groups (like class year), then randomly sample within each group
- Cluster: randomly pick whole groups (like dorms) and survey everyone in them
- Systematic: every 10th person on a list
- Convenience sample: whoever's easy to reach. Usually bad.

## Types of bias

- Selection bias: the way you pick people leaves some groups out. Surveying only people in the library about study habits.
- Voluntary response bias: only people with strong opinions respond. Online reviews, call-in polls.
- Nonresponse bias: the people who don't answer are different from the people who do.
- Response bias: people lie or get nudged by how the question is worded. "Don't you agree that…"
- Undercoverage: part of the population isn't on the list at all.

Famous example: the 1936 Literary Digest poll predicted Landon would beat FDR. It had 2.4 million responses but sampled from phone books and car registrations (wealthier people during the Depression), and only about a quarter of people mailed it back. A huge sample doesn't fix a biased one.
