# Enzymes

Enzymes are proteins that speed up reactions by lowering the activation energy. They don't get used up and they don't change whether a reaction is favorable, just how fast it happens.

## Active site and specificity

The substrate binds at the active site. The old model was "lock and key" but the better model is induced fit: the enzyme changes shape slightly to grip the substrate more tightly. Each enzyme works on one substrate or a small family of them.

## What affects enzyme activity

- Temperature: activity goes up as it gets warmer, until the enzyme denatures (loses its shape) and activity crashes. Human enzymes peak around 37°C.
- pH: each enzyme has an optimal pH. Pepsin in the stomach likes pH ~2, trypsin in the small intestine likes ~8.
- Substrate concentration: more substrate means a faster rate until all the active sites are busy (saturation, Vmax).

We tested the temperature one in lab with catalase from potatoes, see the lab report.

## Inhibitors

- Competitive: looks like the substrate and blocks the active site. Adding more substrate can outcompete it.
- Noncompetitive: binds somewhere else (an allosteric site) and changes the enzyme's shape. More substrate doesn't help.
- Feedback inhibition: the end product of a pathway shuts off an early enzyme. Like a thermostat for metabolism.
