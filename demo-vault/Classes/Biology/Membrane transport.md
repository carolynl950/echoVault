# Membrane transport

The cell membrane is a phospholipid bilayer: hydrophilic heads face out, hydrophobic tails face in. Small nonpolar things (O2, CO2) slip right through. Ions and big polar molecules need help.

## Passive transport

No energy needed, things move down their concentration gradient.

- Simple diffusion: straight through the bilayer
- Facilitated diffusion: through channel or carrier proteins (glucose, ions)
- Osmosis: diffusion of water across a membrane

## Osmosis and tonicity

Water moves toward the side with more dissolved stuff.

- Hypertonic solution (saltier outside): water leaves, cell shrivels
- Hypotonic solution (less salty outside): water enters, animal cells can burst, plant cells get firm (turgor)
- Isotonic: no net movement

This is why you salt eggplant before cooking it: osmosis pulls water out.

## Active transport

Moves things against their gradient, costs ATP. The sodium-potassium pump moves 3 Na+ out and 2 K+ in per ATP. Endocytosis and exocytosis move big things in and out using vesicles.
