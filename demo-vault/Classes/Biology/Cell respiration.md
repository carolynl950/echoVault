# Cell respiration

Big picture: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy. Most of the energy ends up in ATP. Prof. Okafor kept saying "follow the electrons", the whole thing is a controlled way of moving electrons from glucose down to oxygen so the energy comes out in small steps instead of all at once (which would just be fire).

![[cell-respiration-diagram.png]]

## Glycolysis

- Happens in the cytoplasm, no oxygen needed
- 1 glucose (6 carbons) → 2 pyruvate (3 carbons each)
- Net gain: 2 ATP and 2 NADH
- Uses 2 ATP up front ("investment phase") and makes 4 later ("payoff phase")

## Krebs cycle

Also called the citric acid cycle. Pyruvate gets moved into the mitochondria and turned into acetyl-CoA first (that step releases one CO2). Then acetyl-CoA joins with oxaloacetate to make citrate and the cycle turns.

Per glucose (two turns): 2 ATP, 6 NADH, 2 FADH2, and 4 CO2. The CO2 we breathe out comes from here, not from the oxygen we breathe in, which surprised me.

The point of the Krebs cycle isn't really the ATP. It's loading up NADH and FADH2 to carry electrons to the next stage.

## Electron transport chain

Lives in the inner mitochondrial membrane. NADH and FADH2 drop off their electrons, the electrons get passed down a chain of proteins, and each handoff pumps H+ ions into the space between the membranes. That builds up a gradient, like water behind a dam.

The H+ ions flow back through ATP synthase, which spins like a tiny turbine and attaches phosphate to ADP. This is called chemiosmosis. Oxygen is the final electron acceptor at the end of the chain and becomes water. No oxygen means the chain backs up and stops.

This stage makes about 26–28 ATP, so the vast majority. Total per glucose is "about 30–32" in our textbook (older books say 36–38).

## Fermentation

When there's no oxygen, cells can still run glycolysis if they recycle NADH back to NAD+. That's what fermentation does. It makes no extra ATP by itself.

- Lactic acid fermentation: muscles during a sprint, also yogurt
- Alcohol fermentation: yeast, makes ethanol + CO2. The CO2 bubbles are why bread dough puffs up (see [[Sourdough starter]])

Exam tip: fermentation = glycolysis + a step to regenerate NAD+. Only 2 ATP per glucose total.
