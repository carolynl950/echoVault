# Photosynthesis

Basically cell respiration in reverse: CO2 + water + light energy → glucose + O2. Happens in chloroplasts.

## Light reactions

In the thylakoid membranes. Chlorophyll absorbs light (mostly red and blue, it reflects green, which is why leaves look green). The energy splits water, and that's where the oxygen gas comes from. The O2 is basically a waste product.

Products that move on to the next stage: ATP and NADPH. There's an electron transport chain here too, with ATP synthase, same idea as in mitochondria. Okafor called this "the same machine, used twice".

## Calvin cycle

In the stroma (the fluid around the thylakoids). Doesn't need light directly but needs the ATP and NADPH from the light reactions.

1. Carbon fixation: the enzyme rubisco attaches CO2 to RuBP
2. Reduction: ATP and NADPH turn it into G3P (a 3-carbon sugar)
3. Regeneration: most G3P is recycled back into RuBP

It takes 3 turns to export one G3P, and 2 G3P make one glucose. Rubisco is supposedly the most abundant protein on Earth, and it's also kind of bad at its job because it sometimes grabs O2 instead of CO2 (photorespiration).

## C4 and CAM plants

Workarounds for hot, dry places where plants close their stomata to save water.

- C4 (corn, sugarcane): fix CO2 in one cell, then do the Calvin cycle in another. Separated in space.
- CAM (cacti, pineapple): open stomata at night, store CO2, then do the Calvin cycle during the day. Separated in time.
