I keep mixing these up so here's the comparison in one place.

Mitosis is for growth and repair. One division, two daughter cells, genetically identical to the parent, diploid (2n). Skin cells, the cells healing a cut, etc.

Meiosis is for making gametes (sperm and eggs). Two divisions, four daughter cells, each haploid (n), and each one genetically different. The variety comes from two things: crossing over in prophase I (homologous chromosomes swap pieces) and independent assortment in metaphase I (which side each pair lines up on is random).

Phases of mitosis, mnemonic PMAT:
- Prophase: chromosomes condense, spindle forms
- Metaphase: chromosomes line up in the middle
- Anaphase: sister chromatids pulled apart
- Telophase: two nuclei form, then cytokinesis splits the cell

The trick for meiosis: meiosis I separates homologous pairs (that's the reduction step, 2n → n). Meiosis II separates sister chromatids, like mitosis does.

Nondisjunction = chromosomes fail to separate correctly, so a gamete ends up with an extra or missing chromosome. Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome) is the example from lecture.
