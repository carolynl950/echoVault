Gilded Age, roughly 1870 to 1900. Mark Twain's name for it: gold on the outside, rotten underneath.

Huge industrial growth: railroads (the transcontinental railroad was finished in 1869), steel, oil. Carnegie built a steel empire using vertical integration (owning every step from the mines to the mills). Rockefeller's Standard Oil used horizontal integration (buying up competitors) and controlled around 90% of oil refining at one point. Robber barons or captains of industry depending on who you ask. Carnegie wrote "The Gospel of Wealth" saying the rich should give their money away, and then funded about 2,500 libraries.

Workers had it rough: 12-hour days, child labor, dangerous factories. Unions formed: the Knights of Labor (open to almost everyone), then the AFL (skilled workers only, Samuel Gompers). Big strikes: the Great Railroad Strike of 1877, Haymarket in 1886, Homestead in 1892, Pullman in 1894. The government usually sided with the companies.

Immigration exploded, mostly from Southern and Eastern Europe through Ellis Island. Cities grew fast and tenements were packed. Jacob Riis's photos in "How the Other Half Lives" showed it. Political machines like Tammany Hall traded help for immigrants for votes.

The Sherman Antitrust Act (1890) was supposed to break up monopolies but was barely enforced at first. Ironically it was used against unions.
