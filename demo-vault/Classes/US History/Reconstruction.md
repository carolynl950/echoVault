# Reconstruction (1865–1877)

The question after the Civil War: how do the Confederate states rejoin, and what happens to four million freed people?

## Presidential vs Radical Reconstruction

Lincoln's Ten Percent Plan was lenient: a state could rejoin once 10% of voters swore loyalty. After the assassination, Andrew Johnson was even more lenient and pardoned a lot of Confederate leaders. Southern states passed Black Codes that restricted freed people's work and movement, which made Northern voters angry.

The Radical Republicans in Congress (Thaddeus Stevens, Charles Sumner) took over in 1867. The Reconstruction Acts split the South into five military districts, and states had to ratify the 14th Amendment to get back in. Johnson was impeached in 1868 and missed being removed by one vote.

## The Reconstruction Amendments

- 13th (1865): abolished slavery, except as punishment for a crime
- 14th (1868): citizenship for everyone born here, equal protection, due process
- 15th (1870): voting can't be denied because of race (but said nothing about women, which split the suffrage movement)

## Freedmen's Bureau

Set up schools (Howard University, Fisk), helped negotiate labor contracts, and tried to reunite families. It was underfunded and shut down in 1872. "Forty acres and a mule" mostly never happened, and many freed people ended up sharecropping, which kept them in debt to landowners.

## End of Reconstruction

Violence from the KKK and other groups, Northern exhaustion, and the Panic of 1873 all weakened support. The Compromise of 1877 settled the disputed Hayes–Tilden election: Hayes became president and federal troops left the South.

What came after: Jim Crow laws, poll taxes, literacy tests, and grandfather clauses that shut Black men out of voting. In Plessy v. Ferguson (1896) the Supreme Court approved "separate but equal", which stood until Brown v. Board of Education in 1954.

My thesis idea for the essay: Reconstruction didn't fail on its own, it was abandoned.
