Notes from the free knife skills class at the community center.

Hold the knife with a pinch grip: thumb and index finger pinch the blade right in front of the handle, and the other fingers wrap around the handle. It feels weird at first but gives way more control than holding just the handle.

The guiding hand makes a "claw": fingertips curled under, knuckles touching the side of the blade. The knife can't cut what's tucked away.

A sharp knife is safer than a dull one, because a dull one slips off food instead of cutting into it. Honing (the steel rod) just straightens the edge. Sharpening actually removes metal and should only happen a few times a year.

Cuts:
- Dice: cubes (small ≈ 1/4 inch, medium ≈ 1/2 inch)
- Mince: as small as possible, for garlic and herbs
- Julienne: thin matchsticks
- Chiffonade: stack leaves, roll them up, slice thin ribbons. For basil.

Onion trick: cut it in half through the root, leave the root end on so it holds together, make horizontal and vertical cuts, then slice across.
