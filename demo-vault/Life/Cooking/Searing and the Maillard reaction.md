# Searing and the Maillard reaction

The brown crust on seared steak, toast, and roasted coffee is the Maillard reaction: amino acids and sugars reacting at high heat (roughly above 140°C / 285°F) and making hundreds of new flavor compounds. Caramelization is different, that's just sugar breaking down by itself.

## Getting a good sear

1. Dry the surface really well with paper towels. Water can't get hotter than 100°C, so a wet surface steams instead of browning.
2. Get the pan very hot first (cast iron or stainless, not nonstick). Use oil with a high smoke point.
3. Don't crowd the pan. Too much food drops the temperature and everything steams. Cook in batches.
4. Don't move it! Let it release by itself. If it sticks, it isn't ready to flip.

Searing doesn't "seal in juices", that's a myth. It's purely for flavor.

## Bonus: the fond

The brown bits stuck to the pan after searing are called fond. Deglaze with a splash of broth or wine and scrape them up, then you have a pan sauce.
