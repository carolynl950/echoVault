# The Great Gatsby, F. Scott Fitzgerald (1925)

## Nick as narrator

Nick says early on that he's "inclined to reserve all judgments", then judges everyone for the rest of the book. He's clearly biased in Gatsby's favor. He admires him and kind of protects him in the telling, and he leaves out or softens things (the bootlegging, the Wolfsheim connections). So everything we know about Gatsby is filtered through someone who wants us to like him. My book club argued for an hour about whether that makes Nick unreliable or just human.

## The green light

The light at the end of Daisy's dock. At first it stands for Daisy, then more broadly for the future Gatsby is reaching for that keeps moving away. The last page ties it to the whole American Dream: "boats against the current."

## Old money vs new money

East Egg (the Buchanans, inherited money) vs West Egg (Gatsby, self-made, flashy). Tom and Daisy can be careless because their money protects them, they "retreat back into their money" and let other people clean up.

## The valley of ashes

The gray industrial wasteland between the Eggs and the city, watched over by the faded billboard eyes of Doctor T. J. Eckleburg. The cost of everyone else's glitter.
