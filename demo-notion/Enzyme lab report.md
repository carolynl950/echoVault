## Question

How does temperature affect the rate of catalase activity?

## Hypothesis

The reaction rate will increase with temperature up to about 37°C and then drop sharply as the enzyme denatures.

## Method

Catalase came from blended raw potato. We soaked filter paper disks in the potato extract, dropped them into 3% hydrogen peroxide at five temperatures (4, 22, 37, 50, 70°C), and timed how long each disk took to float back up. Faster float = more O2 bubbles = more enzyme activity. Three trials per temperature.

## Results

| Temp (°C) | Avg float time (s) |
| --- | --- |
| 4 | 31.2 |
| 22 | 14.8 |
| 37 | 9.6 |
| 50 | 12.1 |
| 70 | no float after 60 s |

## Discussion

Activity peaked at 37°C, which supports the hypothesis. At 70°C the disks never floated, meaning the catalase denatured and stopped working. One error source: the water bath at 50°C drifted by a few degrees. Next time we'd use more trials and a narrower range around the peak (30–45°C) to find the optimum more precisely.
